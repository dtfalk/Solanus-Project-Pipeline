## Key findings
- Michigan has NO post-mortem right of publicity statute; common-law misappropriation rights generally do NOT survive death; one federal court recognized a post-mortem common-law right but did not fix duration -> deceased Michiganders are legally vulnerable [2][3]
- Solanus Casey died 1957 in Detroit, Michigan; beatified 2017; so primary domicile law (Michigan) is weakest US regime for post-mortem control [3][5]
- The Province of St. Joseph of the Capuchin Order, Inc. (501c3, EIN 38-1525161) holds "all rights reserved" to Solanus digital content; administers Solanus Casey Center, Father Solanus Guild, and the canonization cause via vice-postulator [4][5]
- As a BEATUS (not yet canonized), public cultus is restricted to authorized dioceses/the Capuchin institute; unauthorized public veneration is illicit and "harms the possibility for Canonization" -> images/representations carry cause-sensitivity [6]
- Antiqua et Nova (DDF + Dicastery for Culture, 28 Jan 2025): AI must respect intrinsic human dignity, remain a tool not a substitute for the person; only the human person is morally responsible [7]
- EU AI Act Art. 50(4): deployers creating deepfakes MUST disclose content is artificially generated/manipulated; Art. 50(2): providers must mark synthetic outputs machine-readable; effective 2 Aug 2026; artistic-works exception still requires disclosure that doesn't hamper enjoyment [1][19]
- EU Code of Practice on Transparency (first draft 17 Dec 2025, final ~June 2026) proposes a standardized "AI" icon for AI-generated/meaningfully-edited content [18][19]
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (eff. 1 Jul 2024) is first US law explicitly protecting VOICE against AI cloning; also targets tool/system providers, not just creators [8][9]
- NO FAKES Act of 2025 (HR 2794, reintroduced Apr 2025) would create a FEDERAL post-mortem digital-replica right: 10-yr base term, renewable to 70 yrs if commercially exploited; requires written consent; broad industry support but First Amendment criticism [10][11]
- C2PA / Content Credentials is the open provenance standard (spec 2.2, May 2025); NSA/CISA endorse it for multimedia integrity; but it records only what creators declare (voluntary) [12][13]
- Roadrunner (Bourdain) AI voice: criticized because it generated him SPEAKING words he only wrote, without clear disclosure or family consent ("ethically f***ed up"; ex-wife denied approval) -> the canonical cautionary case [14]
- Dali Lives / Ask Dali: praised as engaging museum innovation BUT criticized for "ventriloquizing the dead" by generating new/contemporary statements via an actor + AI, blurring authentic vs synthetic [15][17]
- Dimensions in Testimony (USC Shoah) is the PRAISED model: NO generative synthesis — it retrieves real pre-recorded answers given with informed consent by the living subject; transparent, grounded, no put-words-in-mouth [16]
- Scholarship: ~58% support digital resurrection only with prior explicit consent of the deceased; only ~3% support it absent consent; "post-mortem privacy void" in most jurisdictions [20]
- Holocaust-testimony ethics literature warns digital duplicates risk authenticity erosion and answering questions the subject never addressed [21]

## Gaps

- No Vatican/USCCB document found that SPECIFICALLY addresses AI recreation of saints/beati (Antiqua et Nova is general) -> gap; analogize from non cultus + dignity principles
