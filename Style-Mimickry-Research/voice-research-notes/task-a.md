# Task A — Restoring/Enhancing Old, Noisy, Band-Limited Disc Speech (2026 SOTA)

AS_OF: 2026-06-22. Context: single ~1945 home acetate/lacquer disc, <5 min total, seconds-to-minutes of connected speech, soft diphtheria-damaged voice, noisy + band-limited; goal is restore BEFORE voice cloning.

## Findings
- iZotope's recommended restoration order is De-clip -> azimuth -> De-click/crackle -> De-hum -> Spectral De-noise -> Voice De-noise, with the principle "tackle the deepest damage first" because clipping is destroyed (not just masked) data and later steps magnify it [1].
- Run De-crackle AFTER De-click for disc material, since decrackle is most effective once the worst impulsive clicks are gone, and crackle/click removal must precede broadband denoise so noise tools "work with the cleanest possible audio" [1][2].
- Aggressive spectral denoise at ~18 dB+ reduction produces "hollow"/"phasey" artifacts; two gentler passes are advised over one aggressive pass [1].
- CEDAR Declick (the first pro digital restoration tool, ~1990) plus Decrackle and Dethump are the commercial archival standard explicitly for 78s, cylinders, acetate/optical soundtracks; all commercial, not open-source [3].
- VoiceFixer is the first unified model to jointly denoise + dereverb + declip + super-resolve, expanding arbitrarily low-bandwidth input up to 44.1 kHz, and is reported effective on real historical/old speech recordings [4].
- AudioSR (MIT) is a diffusion model that upsamples any input in the 2 kHz–16 kHz bandwidth to a 24 kHz-bandwidth signal at 48 kHz sample rate, for speech/music/general audio [5].
- The 2026 BWE survey targets output rates of 16/22.05/24/44.1/48/96/192 kHz (VCTK@48 kHz is the de-facto speech benchmark) but stresses BWE is an "ill-posed reconstruction": missing high-frequency content is not uniquely determined and generative outputs are "stochastic and may not align sample-by-sample with the reference" [6].
- Resemble Enhance (MIT) runs a UNet denoiser then a latent conditional-flow-matching enhancer trained on 44.1 kHz speech that restores distortions and extends bandwidth; DeepFilterNet (MIT/Apache-2.0) is a low-complexity full-band 48 kHz real-time denoiser; NVIDIA CleanUNet is a waveform-domain self-attention denoiser [7][8][9].
- A 2026 study (VocalBridge) shows cleaning protected speech before cloning restores speaker-verification 28–45% and voice-conversion attack success >60%, and listeners rated cleaned audio as preserving identity ("sounded similar … outperformed other cleanup methods") — i.e., denoise/purification preserves enough timbre to clone [10][11].
- Adobe Podcast Enhance is generative re-synthesis ("hallucinates good audio" via transcribe-then-respeak) and can introduce robotic timbre, lisped S/T sibilants, and muffled clip edges — a clear risk of inventing timbre for a clone reference [12].

## Deep Read Notes

### [1] iZotope — Order of Operations (official, Authority 9)
Canonical workflow ordering with reasoning. Recommended sequence: (1) De-clip, (2) azimuth/phase, (3) De-click and Mouth De-click, (4) De-hum, (5) Spectral De-noise (steady broadband), (6) Voice De-noise (variable: breaths, sibilance). Core rule: "always try to tackle the deepest damage first," where "deepest" = most pervasive across the file, not most audible. Clipping is first because the chopped-off waveform "is gone, and nothing can put it back," and downstream steps magnify it. Clicks before broadband noise so denoise modules get the cleanest input. Explicit artifact warning: Spectral De-noise at ~18 dB+ yields "hollow"/"phasey" artifacts; prefer two light passes; the chain is "easy to undo and try again," so iterate. Note for our case: this implies bandwidth extension/super-resolution should be a LAST, separate generative step after declick+denoise, since it is reconstructive rather than corrective.

### [6] 2026 Audio Super-Resolution / BWE Survey (academic, Authority 8)
Frames bandwidth extension as inherently ill-posed: "missing HF content is not uniquely determined by the input," so one degraded input maps to many plausible outputs. Discriminative models (direct LR->HR mapping) suffer "regression-to-the-mean" and "spectral over-smoothing" (dull but faithful); generative models (diffusion/flow/bridge: AudioSR, FlashSR, NU-Wave 2, mdctGAN, AERO, FLowHigh, UniverSR, CodecFlow 2026) model a distribution of plausible HF and sound sharper, but "generated HF components are stochastic and may not align sample-by-sample with the reference." Practical reading for a 1945 disc: any recovered high frequencies above the disc's real cutoff are model-invented, not recorded — perceptually plausible but NOT the actual person's recorded spectrum. Targets span 16–192 kHz; speech work centers on 48 kHz (VCTK benchmark).

### [10]/[11] VocalBridge — cleanup before cloning (secondary-industry + academic, Authority 6/7)
A 2026 latent-diffusion "purification" system that strips adversarial anti-cloning noise from speech while preserving speaker identity (operates on compressed/latent representations to separate added noise from natural speech). Results: restored speaker verification 28–45% across five protection tools; voice-conversion attack success >60% in some cases; even adaptive defenses accepted >75% of cleaned samples; >75% of listeners rated cleaned/cloned samples "acceptable or better"; adding timing cues improved identity recovery >10 pts. Takeaway directly for us: identity-preserving DENOISING/purification helps downstream cloning rather than hurting it — the speaker's timbre survives non-generative cleanup. The implied caution is the inverse: generative RE-SYNTHESIS (vocoder/respeak enhancers) is the part that risks substituting timbre, not the denoise step.

## Gaps
- No source gave a hard sample-rate CEILING specific to a 1945 home acetate; realistic recorded content is roughly band-limited to ~mid-single-kHz cut-off (home cutters/discs of the era), so anything reconstructed above the disc's true cutoff via AudioSR/VoiceFixer is model-invented, not the real voice — I could not find a measured per-format number for home acetates.
- I found NO controlled study isolating "restore-then-clone of a real degraded historical voice" with a same-speaker identity metric; the cloning-helps evidence [10][11] is from anti-spoof/adversarial-noise removal, not shellac/acetate restoration, so it transfers only by analogy.
- The strongest documented identity RISK is generative re-synthesis enhancers (Adobe Podcast [12]; vocoder-based VoiceFixer [4]) "hallucinating" timbre/sibilants; I could not find a peer-reviewed measurement of how much such hallucination corrupts a clone reference for a SOFT/atypical (e.g., diphtheria-damaged) voice — atypical voices are likely worse than benchmark VCTK speakers, but this is unquantified.
- Honest uncertainty: best-supported strategy is conservative, mostly-discriminative cleanup (declick/decrackle then mild denoise) to feed the clone, and to KEEP an un-super-resolved version, because generative bandwidth extension/respeak may add plausible-but-fake high-frequency timbre that the cloner will learn as if it were the real person.
