Father Solanus Casey (1870-1957), a Capuchin friar, is consistently identified in our archive by his signature "Fr. Solanus, O.F.M.Cap." and his association with the Seraphic Mass Association. His early ministry saw him move through various locations, beginning in West Superior, Wisconsin, in 1896, then to Milwaukee in 1904. By 1915, he was serving in Brooklyn, followed by Yonkers, New York, from 1916 to 1917, and later in New York City through the early 1920s.

A significant part of Father Solanus's life was spent in Detroit, Michigan, where he served as a porter at St. Bonaventure Monastery from at least 1928, returning there again in the 1940s after brief periods in Brighton, Michigan, and Glenclyffe, New York. His archive reveals his deep pastoral care, as he offered prayers for health to many petitioners, including Richard and Mrs. E. J. Werthman in 1935 and 1948. He also maintained personal connections, corresponding with his brother, Owen B. J. Casey, and recounting visits that included a high Mass and a "conference with the prisoners of Jesus," reflecting his compassionate ministry to the marginalized.

The breadth of Father Solanus's connections is evident in his extensive correspondence with individuals such as Bonaventure Frey, Mrs. Ella Traynor, Mother Lurana White, and various Sisters of the Atonement, as well as with publications like the *Catholic News* and the *Irish World*. People from all walks of life sought his prayers, specifically for conditions like cancer, operations, blindness, sickness, fever, paralysis, tumors, infections, deafness, and other diseases. Petitions also frequently requested prayers for those struggling with drink and consumption, highlighting the diverse needs he addressed. Father Solanus Casey's life of service, as documented in these records, led to his beatification in 2017.
